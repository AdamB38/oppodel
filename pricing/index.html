Honest answers

The questions installers actually ask

What is this, exactly?

It's a page your customers use themselves. They put in their address, take a photo of the house, and pick what they want lit — roofline, trees, wraps — and the price builds live as they go. When they're happy, they book. The first you hear about it is a quote and a photo of the house landing on your phone.

I haven't got a website. Can I still use it?

Yes — and it's arguably more useful to you than to someone who has one. You get a link. It goes in your Facebook replies, your text messages, your Google listing, or a QR code on the truck. Someone asks what lights would cost at nine at night and you send the link instead of losing your Saturday to a drive‑out. If you do have a site, it sits on your domain as its own page or a button. Either way I set it up.

Do I need a CRM or any other software?

No. Plenty of installers run the whole season off their phone and that works fine here — quotes come through by text and email. If you do use something like Jobber or Housecall Pro, I'll wire it in so leads land there automatically as new requests. You don't set any of that up.

There are tools at $50 a month. Why is this different?

There are, and some of them are decent — if one covers what you need, genuinely, use it and keep your money. Two things are different here. The first is that a template looks like a template: the same widget every other company has, bolted onto your site. Yours is built to match your branding — your colours, your fonts, your logo — so the customer never feels like they've been handed off to someone else's software. The second is where the price comes from. A template prices off one flat rate you type in, so a steep pitch or an awkward access job hands your customer a number you then have to ring up and correct. Yours is built around your services and your rates, from your own past jobs. No two of these businesses price the same way, so no two builds are the same.

Why is there a build fee at all?

Because you're not being handed a login. The $1,199 is the work of turning how you actually price a roofline — your rates, your minimums, your access and height loading, the calls you make without thinking — into something a homeowner can use on their phone at 11pm. A template doesn't need a build fee because nobody built anything.

What does it cost?

$1,199 to build it, then $59 a month maintenance for twelve months — $1,907 for your first year, all in. One booked job covers the build; two cover the whole year. There are no add-ons, no per-quote charges and no setup extras: the two numbers on this page are the two numbers.

What does my customer actually see?

A page with your name on it and a photo of their own house with the lights drawn on it — not a form. That matters more than it sounds. Most quotes in this trade arrive as a number in a text message; yours arrives looking like it came from a company with an office.

Who else is using it?

In Christmas lights, nobody yet — this is its first season and you'd be one of the first on it. I'm not going to dress that up. What I would say is that I ran one of these businesses myself, so this isn't a software company guessing at how your October works.

What do you need from me?

Your pricing, mostly. Ideally last season's jobs — what you charged, roof type, how many storeys, rough footage. If you haven't kept records we do it from memory: what you'd charge for a standard single‑storey ranch, a two‑storey colonial and a big one. I build from those and we tune it together before it goes live.

How accurate is the measuring?

It calibrates from their front door (a known size) and cross-checks against a satellite trace of their roof. It's a bidding estimate — accurate enough to price confidently, and your crew confirms final footage before install, exactly like you do today. The live demo is open if you want to see it measure a real house before we talk.

Does the AI render show things the customer didn't buy?

No — every render is checked by a second AI that compares it against exactly what the customer drew. Extra lights, wrong colors, or a wrong house get rejected and re-generated automatically.

Am I the only one in my area?

Yes — one installer per metro. If I build your pricing logic and then build it for the company across town, I've effectively handed him how you price. So your metro is either yours or it isn't.

September 2026, ahead of quote season. Builds run in the order enquiries come in, and a build takes about two weeks from getting your pricing — but quote season starts in October, so the earlier you start the more of the season you get.

Do I pay anything to enquire?

No. No card is taken and nothing is charged for getting in touch. You'll have the full terms in writing before anything is due, and you can walk away at that point.
